## Chapter 1 From the spotless black Bentley, I stepped out. The cool Manhattan night air brushed against me. The shimmering city skyline scoffed at my restlessness: Gone were the days when a night out to yet another high-society event demanded that I play the part of a perfect mafia princess. Something about the thought sent shivers down my spine. My entrance into the Plaza Hotel ballroom initiated a spectacle with the gazes of a hundred onlookers being held, a sensation I had never experienced before. The glass chandeliers would bathe the scene in glorious radiance: the splendid backdrop of the sea with designer gowns and tailored suits showcased under an artist's hand, only I felt the warm rays meant to spotlight my every move. I scanned the room for some kind of acquaintance or a friend in that mass of Malice and Viciousness that New York City could muster. My eyes landed upon my cousin, Giordanna, and I walked over to her, hoping for a somewhat merciful reprieve from the stifling atmosphere in the place. Having grown up witnessing this via her eyes, she was one among our cadre of old friends."Giordanna smiled mischievously: "Val, you look stunning. I was beginning to think you'd sneak out on us tonight." I let out a weak smile while accepting a glass of bubbly from a passing waiter. "Trust me, that thought did occur to me again," I said, sipping while enjoying the bubbles bursting against my tongue. "I just don't know any other way to take this, Gia." Giordanna's face softened, and her eyes filled with understanding. "Surely it isn't easy, but you know how much these events mean to Uncle Sal. To the family…" "The family expects me to be here, I know," I interrupted, with bitterness in my voice. "The family expects me to smile, to charm, to be their perfect little pawn. When am I going to get to live for myself?" Giordanna looked around nervously to make sure no one was within eavesdropping. "Val, be careful. You know how dangerous it is to even think about leaving." I sighed and ran my hand through my carefully styled hair. "No, Gia, I'm not talking about leaving. I want more than this. More than being paraded around like a trophy, more than being married off as a broodmare to strengthen alliances. Is that too much to ask?" Before Giordanna could respond, there was a loud noise near the entrance, and as the crowd began to pull away, I saw him. He moved around the room like a predator; his presence was felt, and he commanded attention with each step. His dark eyes scanned the crowd, sharp and calculated, as if he were assessing every person in his way. He looked dangerous, like a man who could destroy anything or anyone that stood in his way. The perfectly tailored suit only enhanced the raw masculinity that seemed to radiate from him; at every point, he exuded authority and control. "Who is that?" I breathed in a whisper, unable to stop staring, my pulse racing faster with every second he seemed to be moving nearer to me. Giordanna followed my line of sight; her lips curled into a smile implying much. "That's Aldo Cavalli. He is new to town, just arrived from Chicago. Rumour has it that he intends to expand his business interests, but what exactly those are... well, that depends on who you ask." Aldo was moved through the crowd. Exchanging pleasantries with a few men, whose names I barely registered. He was carrying himself with tremendous confidence while underneath, something dark. His smile has charm, but his eyes are empty. It's as though the predator is amusing itself by gently testing its prey. There was something that made my breath get caught in my throat, a danger I dreaded but was irrationally attracted to. My heart pounded as he drew near. For some reason, half of me wanted to see what would unfold between those eyes and me. Val, be careful; I am warning you, those men are nothing but trouble. I turned to her, full of defiance. "Maybe what I need is trouble. Before I could second-guess myself, I handed my empty glass to Giordanna and moved across the room. My heart pounded with each step, a strange mix of excitement and uncertainty. It felt as if I was walking straight into something dangerous. Still, there was something about him that drew me in, made me want to see just how close I could get before it all went wrong. Aldo’s eyes found mine as I approached, and suddenly the entire room seemed to fall away, leaving just us. Up close, he was even more striking than I’d realized, with sharp features, a strong jaw, and those dark eyes that made it impossible to hide anything. There was more to him than just being attractive; he had a sense of danger that I couldn’t ignore, no matter how much I told myself to be careful. “Mr. Cavalli,” I said, keeping my voice steady even as nerves raced through me. I held out my hand, aware of how close we stood. “I don’t believe we’ve met. I’m Valeria Moretti.” His hand closed around mine, his grip firm and his skin noticeably warm. “The pleasure is mine, Ms. Moretti,” he said, his voice both smooth and serious. “I’ve heard a lot about you.” I let out a small chuckle. “All good things, I hope?” He gave a slight smile in return. “Depends who you ask. Some say you’re the jewel of your family. Others say you’re more trouble than you’re worth.” His words were bold, but I refused to look away. “And you? What do you think?” He paused for a moment, eyes studying me, and then said, “The truth is probably somewhere in the middle. I’d like to find out for myself.” There was a tension in the air between us, something that pulled at me and made it impossible to look away. I felt a rush of adrenaline I hadn’t felt in years, knowing this could lead somewhere I might regret, but an undeniable part of me wanted to see where it would go. As I turned to walk, I felt his hand rest lightly on my back, a simple gesture. There was nothing forceful in his touch, just a calm certainty. A shiver ran through me, but I kept moving, aware of his presence with every step. That gentle control, the way he seemed to guide me without a word, was unexpected and left me quietly unsettled. Still, I let him steer, uncertain but drawn forward by curiosity. We stepped out into the night air, city lights shining over Manhattan. “Where to?” he asked, voice low. I turned, a mix of excitement and fear in my chest. “Surprise me,” I said. As we got into the waiting car, I understood I was crossing a line. But with Aldo’s eyes on me, I didn’t want to stop. The car, the driver, and the expensive hotel room all belonged to the FBI. I was just playing a part: the outsider, wealthy and interesting enough for the Morettis to let me in. Every piece of it was designed to sell that story. Valeria sat next to me, legs crossed, watching the city blur by. There was a tension in the car that didn’t need words; her presence was strong even in silence. I caught the faint scent of jasmine, familiar, but edged with something sharper. She seemed both confident and unpredictable. I noticed the way she sat, the way she moved, as if she was always aware of the room and how much space she occupied. I stuck to the role, every gesture and word considered. She was my way in, the person I needed to get close to for this to work. But there was something about her that kept me on edge, something that made me pay more attention than I should’ve. I couldn’t let myself forget why I was there, no matter how much she drew me in. I couldn't afford that, not with Valeria. This job was supposed to be straightforward. Get close, get information, help take down the Morettis. But with her, it was different. She wasn’t just another target. I watched Valeria from the corner of my vision, careful not to let my attention linger too long. Up close, she made an impression; her features were sharp, her lips parted as if she were lost in thought. People liked to call her the mafia princess, but I could tell there was more to her than that. She carried herself with quiet confidence, never flinching or shrinking back, even when I touched her back. She seemed used to attention, maybe even expected it, but she never gave away control. That was important. She wasn’t here for me; she was the reason I was here, my assignment. Every move mattered; she was my way into the Moretti family and everything connected to them. The badge in my pocket was a constant reminder: this was about the job, not about getting distracted by her presence. There was an unpredictability to her, something simmering underneath the calm. She met my eyes directly back at the Plaza, no hesitation. Most people would have looked away or tried to soften the moment. Not her. She met the challenge. That’s what set her apart. She was never going to be easy to read or control. It was clear she’d push back if I tried. Still, I had to stay focused. She was my entry point, nothing more. Yet, I couldn’t help but wonder what was beneath that calm, how much of it was real. The pull was there, but I couldn’t let it cloud my judgment. Not with Valeria. She was important, and I couldn’t afford to forget it. But damn, she was intoxicating. “There’s a spot nearby,” I said, keeping my tone steady even if my mind was busy. “Quiet. We can talk.” She turned, meeting my eyes with a small, unreadable smile. I couldn’t tell if it was real or for show. With Valeria Moretti, it was probably a bit of both. She always seemed a step ahead, and she knew I realized that. City lights blurred past as we drove, and the air between us grew heavier. She shifted closer, fingers tapping quietly on her leg. There was an unmistakable tension. She seemed ready for something, and for once, I wasn’t sure if I was prepared. “Lead the way, Mr. Cavalli,” she said, her voice calm but with an edge. We stopped in front of a brownstone, the car engine fading to silence. Outside noise drifted back in: distant voices, a horn, and faint music from inside. I glanced at Valeria. She seemed comfortable, as if she belonged here. She understood this wasn’t just an ordinary bar. We entered through an unmarked door, a guard outside giving us a quick look. My hand found the small of her back as we walked in, almost without thinking. Inside, the design was simple and quiet: dark wood, leather, warm lighting. The kind of place where people talked in low voices and didn’t ask too many questions. I led Valeria to a booth in the corner, keeping an eye on the room. Some habits stick, no matter where you are. She looked around and nodded. “Impressive,” she said. “Didn’t know about this place.” I signaled the waiter and ordered two glasses of Macallan, the thirty-year-old kind, no sense in settling for less. “I make it a habit to learn the hidden corners here,” I said, my voice carrying just enough hint of danger. “Especially the ones with the right sort of privacy.” Valeria’s eyebrow lifted, interest flickering in her eyes. “And what sort would that be, Mr. Cavalli?” I leaned in, lowering my voice. “The kind that values discretion, Ms. Moretti. Cities like this, secrets keep them running.” Our drinks arrived. Valeria took a slow sip, her movements careful and practiced. Beneath the surface, I could sense something more, but I kept my focus. “So,” I asked, glancing around, “what’s it like, growing up surrounded by all this?” I gestured toward the room. “I imagine there are advantages.” Valeria’s expression shifted, just for a second. “Advantages?” she repeated, her tone reserved. “That depends. Some doors open, others close.” I raised an eyebrow, keeping it light. “And the ones that close? That sounds…difficult.” She gave a brief, humorless smile. “It can be. People have their assumptions. Living up to them isn’t always simple.” I leaned back, watching her. “Must be a lot to manage.” For a moment, something unreadable crossed her eyes. “It comes with the territory. Expectations, plans, they don’t stop.” “From your family?” I asked, as if it were a casual question, though we both knew it wasn’t. She paused, then nodded. “From everyone. But yes, especially from family.” I kept that in mind. Every detail mattered. Valeria wasn’t just another guest here; she was my way into the Moretti family. This wasn’t just a conversation; it was an opportunity. The more I saw, the clearer it became that Valeria was more than just a piece in the game. She had intelligence, sure, but also something deeper, resentment that didn’t come from money or comfort. It hinted at real fractures behind the Moretti family’s polished front. The kind of thing the FBI noticed, the kind I needed to get close to if I wanted to do my job. This wasn’t just about getting her attention; it was about earning her trust, slowly, until she’d let me in. I’d done this work before, but Valeria was different. She wasn’t someone I could win over easily, and part of me respected that, even when I shouldn’t. The job was getting complicated. She wasn’t the straightforward target I’d expected. Still, the task remained the same: get in, gather what I needed, and leave. No room for distractions, no matter how much she drew me in. She looked at me, directly. “And what about you? What brings a man like Aldo Cavalli to New York? Wasn’t Chicago enough?” I managed a small smile. “New York seemed like the right place for something new. Plenty of possibilities here.” She didn’t look away. “And what possibilities are you after, Mr. Cavalli?” The conversation hung between us, somewhere between challenge and interest. I found myself wanting to know more about her, even if that wasn’t part of the plan. “The kind that changes things,” I said quietly. “The kind you can’t manage alone.” Valeria’s lips parted, her breath quickening. “And what makes you think I’d want any sort of partnership with you?” I reached out, my fingertips just brushing hers across the table. The touch caught us both off guard. I could see it in her eyes, that brief flash of surprise. “Because, Ms. Moretti,” I said quietly, “I think you’re searching for something outside of what your family expects. And maybe you sense I could help you find it.” She paused, studying me. Then, a small, careful smile appeared, measured, but impossible to ignore. “You’re taking a risk, Mr. Cavalli,” she replied, her voice low. I leaned in a bit, enough to show I wasn’t backing down. “Sometimes those risks pay off.” The tension between us was impossible to miss. I was well aware of the stakes, but right then, I was willing to take the chance. “Maybe we should talk somewhere private?” I suggested, my voice steady. Valeria’s expression shifted, a mix of challenge and curiosity in her eyes. “Your move, Mr. Cavalli,” she said, making it clear the next step was mine.
