Collision Course

## Chapter 1 [Tessa] The bus lurches to a stop, and I'm the first one out, eager to stretch my legs after the long journey. As I step onto the snow-covered ground, my breath catches in my throat. The Colorado Rockies loom before me, majestic and intimidating, their snow-capped peaks seeming to touch the cloudless blue sky. "Holy shit," I whisper, drinking in the view. The mountains are a challenge—a dare—and my heart races with anticipation. This is what I've been training for. "Tessa! Wait up!" Sam's voice calls from behind me. I turn to see my best friend stumbling off the bus, her arms full of gear. "Sorry," I say, reaching out to steady her. "I got a little awestruck." Sam grins, her cheeks already flushed from the cold. "Can't blame you. These mountains are something else." We make our way to the lodge, our boots crunching in the fresh snow. The air is crisp and clean, filled with the scent of pine. It's invigorating, and I feel my spirits lifting with each breath. "So," Sam says, bumping my shoulder. "You ready to show these trust fund babies how it's done?" I laugh, but there's an edge to it. "You know it. They may have the fancy gear, but we've got heart." And determination, I add silently. And the need to prove ourselves. The lodge is a bustle of activity, skiers and organizers milling about. I scan the crowd, half-expecting to see a familiar face—one I'm not quite ready to confront. But he's not here. Not yet. We check in and head to our room. It's small but cozy, with two twin beds and a window overlooking the slopes. I drop my bags and immediately start unpacking, my movements quick and efficient. "Whoa, slow down there, speed racer," Sam jokes, flopping onto her bed. "The slopes aren't going anywhere." I ignore her, focusing on laying out my gear. My hands move over each piece with practiced ease—goggles, gloves, thermal layers. Everything has its place, everything serves a purpose. Control what you can control, my dad always says. "Earth to Tessa," Sam says, waving a hand in front of my face. "You okay? You seem a little... intense." I pause, realizing I've been meticulously arranging and rearranging my ski poles for the last five minutes. "Yeah, I'm fine," I say, not meeting her eyes. "Just want to make sure everything's perfect." Sam sits up, her expression softening. "Is this about the race, or is it about him?" My hands still. "I don't know what you're talking about." "Oh, come on," Sam says, rolling her eyes. "Mr. Tall, Dark, and Broody? The guy who's had you all wound up for years? Ring any bells?" I feel my cheeks heat up, and it's not from the altitude. "Max has nothing to do with this," I say, my voice sharper than I intend. "I'm here to ski. That's all." Sam holds up her hands in surrender. "Okay, okay. Just... don't let him get in your head, alright? You're a kickass skier, Tess. You don't need to prove anything to anyone." I nod, grateful for her support even as doubt gnaws at me. Because the truth is, I do need to prove something. To myself, to the skiing world, and yes, to Max Donovan. As Sam heads to the bathroom to freshen up, I turn my attention to my most prized possession—my skis. They're not the newest model or the most expensive brand, but they're mine. I've poured countless hours and every spare penny into maintaining them, and they've never let me down. I run my fingers along the edges, checking for any nicks or imperfections. The familiar routine soothes me, grounding me in the present. But as I work, my mind drifts back to the last time I saw Max, three years ago at another charity event. The memory hits me with startling clarity. I can still feel the biting cold of that day, see the disapproval in Max's green eyes as he looked down at me—literally and figuratively. "You're going to get yourself killed skiing like that," he had said, his voice as icy as the slopes around us. "It's reckless and unprofessional." I remember the fury that had coursed through me, hot enough to melt the snow at my feet. "And you're going to bore yourself to death skiing like a robot," I had shot back. "Some of us actually enjoy the sport, you know." His jaw had clenched, a muscle ticking in his cheek. "Enjoyment doesn't win races or keep you safe. Discipline does." "Right, because you're such an expert on winning these days," I had sneered, immediately regretting the words as hurt flashed in his eyes. But I was too angry, too proud to take them back. Max had straightened, his expression hardening into a mask of indifference. "Good luck in the race, Ms. Greene," he had said coldly. "Try not to crash and burn." And then he had walked away, leaving me fuming in the snow. I shake my head, dispelling the memory. My hands have stilled on my skis, and I realize I've been gripping them so tightly, my knuckles have turned white. "Get it together, Tessa," I mutter to myself. "He's just another competitor. Nothing more." But even as I say the words, I know they're a lie. Max Donovan has never been just another competitor to me. He's been my rival, my inspiration, my frustration, and sometimes, in the quietest corners of my mind, something else entirely. I finish my equipment check just as Sam emerges from the bathroom. "All set?" she asks, eyeing my meticulously arranged gear. I nod, forcing a smile. "Ready as I'll ever be." We head down to the welcome dinner, the lodge buzzing with excitement. As we enter the dining hall, my eyes automatically scan the crowd. I tell myself I'm just getting a feel for the competition, but deep down, I know who I'm looking for. And then I see him. Max is standing near the fireplace, deep in conversation with one of the event organizers. He looks good—too good. His dark hair is slightly tousled, like he's just taken off his beanie, and his green eyes are intense as he listens to whatever the organizer is saying. As if sensing my gaze, Max looks up. Our eyes lock across the room, and for a moment, everything else fades away. There's surprise in his expression, then something else—something that makes my heart stutter in my chest. And then Sam is pulling me towards the buffet, breaking the connection. "Come on," she says, oblivious to the moment I've just had. "Let's fuel up. You've got some ass to kick tomorrow." I let her lead me away, but I can feel Max's eyes on me as we go. It's going to be a long week, I think, my determination warring with a flutter of anticipation in my stomach. And I'm ready to see what happens. ## Chapter 2 [Max] I step out of the private car, sunglasses firmly in place, and plaster on my best camera-ready smile. The crisp mountain air hits me, carrying with it the scent of pine and snow. For a moment, just a fleeting second, I'm transported back to my competition days. The thrill, the anticipation, the rush of adrenaline before a big race. Then reality crashes back. I'm not here to compete. Not anymore. "Max! Over here!" "Mr. Donovan, can we get a statement?" "Max, how does it feel to be back on the slopes?" The barrage of questions and camera flashes assault me, and I fall back on years of media training. Smile, wave, give them just enough to keep them happy without revealing anything real. "It's great to be here," I say, my voice carrying easily over the clamor. "I'm looking forward to supporting this fantastic cause and seeing some world-class skiing." As I make my way through the crowd, signing autographs and posing for selfies, I can't help but scan the faces around me. I tell myself I'm just getting a feel for the event, but deep down, I know who I'm looking for. Who I'm dreading—and maybe hoping—to see. Tessa Greene. The last time I saw her, we'd had a heated argument that left a bitter taste in my mouth. Her words still echo in my mind: "Right, because you're such an expert on winning these days." The memory stings, a sharp reminder of everything I've lost. I push the thought away as I spot James Montgomery making his way towards me. James, the CEO of the company that sponsors me, is all smiles and handshakes as he approaches. "Max! Glad you made it," he says, clapping me on the shoulder. "How was the drive up?" "Long," I admit, allowing a small, genuine smile. "But the views made up for it. These mountains never get old." James nods, his eyes gleaming with barely contained excitement. "Wait until you see the course they've set up. It's going to be one hell of a race." The mention of the race sends a pang through my chest, a mixture of longing and frustration that I quickly suppress. "I'm sure it will be," I say, keeping my tone neutral. "When's the meeting with the organizers?" "Right now, actually," James says, checking his watch. "Shall we?" I nod, grateful for the distraction. As we make our way to the lodge, I can't help but notice the way other skiers and guests turn to stare. Some with admiration, others with pity. I'm not sure which is worse. Inside, the lodge is a hive of activity. Skiers, organizers, and media personnel buzz around, their excitement palpable. The smell of coffee and pine mingles in the air, and for a moment, I'm hit with a wave of nostalgia so strong, it almost knocks me off my feet. "You okay?" James asks, noticing my hesitation. I nod, forcing myself back to the present. "Yeah, just... taking it all in." The meeting room is mercifully quiet compared to the lobby. A group of event organizers is already seated around a large oak table, their faces lighting up as James and I enter. "Mr. Donovan, it's an honor to have you here," one of them, a middle-aged woman with a no-nonsense haircut, says as she stands to shake my hand. "I'm Sarah Winters, head of the organizing committee." "Please, call me Max," I say, shaking her hand firmly. "It's great to be here. You've put together quite an event." As we take our seats, Sarah launches into a detailed explanation of the event schedule, the charity beneficiaries, and the course layout. I listen attentively, asking questions and offering suggestions where appropriate. This is familiar territory—the business side of skiing that I've immersed myself in since my injury. But as Sarah describes the racecourse, I feel a familiar itch in my palms, a restlessness in my legs. The urge to strap on my skis and tackle the mountain myself is almost overwhelming. "And of course, we're thrilled to have you judge the freestyle competition," Sarah says, snapping me back to attention. "Your expertise will be invaluable." I nod, pushing down the twinge of regret. "I'm looking forward to it. We've got some real talent in the lineup this year." "Speaking of talent," James cuts in, leaning forward with a gleam in his eye. "Any standouts you've got your eye on, Max? We're always on the lookout for the next big star." The question catches me off guard, and unbidden, an image of Tessa flashes through my mind—her fierce determination, the way she attacked the slopes with a reckless abandon that both terrified and exhilarated me. "It's... too early to say," I hedge, aware of the expectant looks around the table. "I'll need to see them on the slopes first." James nods, but I can see the disappointment in his eyes. He's been pushing me to find new talent, to prove my worth beyond my own skiing career. It's a pressure I'm not sure I'm ready to handle. The meeting wraps up shortly after, and I find myself being ushered from one commitment to another. Photo ops, interviews, meet-and-greets with sponsors. By the time I finally make it to my suite, I'm exhausted, my public persona slipping away like snow melting in the sun. The suite is luxurious, with floor-to-ceiling windows offering a breathtaking view of the mountains. But my eyes are drawn to the familiar leather case sitting on the dresser. Inside, I know, are my old trophies—a request from the event organizers for a display in the lodge. With a sigh, I open the case. The gold and silver gleam in the fading light, each one a memory of triumph, of pushing myself to the limit and coming out on top. I pick up the largest one, from my last world championship before the injury. The weight of it in my hands brings back a flood of memories: the roar of the crowd, the bite of cold air on my face as I flew down the slope, the indescribable feeling of knowing, even before I crossed the finish line, that I'd won. And then, sharp and vivid, the memory of my last race. The sickening crack as my ski caught, the world spinning out of control, the blinding pain. The long months of surgeries and rehab, only to be told I'd never compete again. I set the trophy down harder than necessary, turning away from the case. My eyes land on the window, on the mountains beyond. Somewhere out there, Tessa Greene is preparing for tomorrow's race. Probably pushing herself to the limit, taking risks that make my heart race just thinking about them. Part of me wants to find her, to warn her about the dangers of skiing the way she does. But I know how that conversation would go. She'd accuse me of being a has-been, of trying to hold her back out of jealousy or bitterness. And maybe she'd be right. A knock at the door startles me out of my thoughts. "Mr. Donovan?" a voice calls. "Your presence is requested at the welcome dinner." I close my eyes, taking a deep breath. Time to put the mask back on. "I'll be right there," I call back, already reaching for my jacket. As I head for the door, I catch a glimpse of myself in the mirror. I look every inch the successful ex-athlete turned businessman. But in my eyes, I can see the shadow of doubt, the longing for what I've lost. I straighten my shoulders, fixing my expression into one of confident ease. It's going to be a long week, I think, as I step out into the hallway. But as I make my way to the elevator, a small part of me can't help but look forward to seeing Tessa again, to feeling that spark of challenge she always ignites in me.
