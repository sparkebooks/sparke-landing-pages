At Last

## Chapter 1 *Sophie* Sophie Chambers stood on her front porch with Dr. Zach Kemper, both his hands wrapped around hers in such a way that she could still see the engagement ring. It was well past midnight by the time they’d arrived at her house, having spent all day and into the night in DC, where Zach had proposed to her. He had shut off his headlights in the driveway so they wouldn’t disturb the neighbors, but the full October moon shone brightly, giving the diamond in her ring a sparkling glow. He could have given her a plastic band from a box of candy, and it still would have looked just as beautiful to her. They’d been through so much together. They’d waited for this moment for so long. She couldn’t wait to be his wife. “I had such a wonderful time,” she said. He squeezed her hand a little tighter. “Well, I certainly hope so. It’s not every day you get engaged on a date.” She chuckled lightly. “No, I suppose not.” “Unfortunately, I have to say goodnight now,” he whispered. She shook her head. “I don’t want to.” It was his turn to chuckle lightly. “It won’t be long before we don’t have to. Most engagements are longer than two months. We only have to wait till Christmas.” “Just another reason I can’t wait for Christmas Eve,” she said. He smiled and moved closer, giving her a soft kiss in the moonlight. She closed her eyes for that moment, but it was over so quickly. “I’d better get going,” he said. “It’s late. You get inside so I don’t have to worry about you.” Sophie nodded, not wanting more words to break the magic of the kiss. She unlocked the door, stepped inside, and shut it lightly, knowing he wouldn’t walk away from the porch until he heard the lock click, even though she lived in a very safe neighborhood. She leaned against the door, turned the deadbolt, and listened as he walked to his car and drove down the street. She felt like she wanted to say a thankful prayer, jump for joy, and run a marathon all at once, her emotions were running so high. She took a few deep breaths and decided to go with the first one, since God had brought so much happiness to her life. “Dear Lord, You have brought so many blessings, and I cannot possibly thank You enough for helping Zach find his way back to me. I promise to live our lives together in a way that always celebrates Your glory every day. I pray in the name of Jesus Christ, my Savior. Amen.” Sophie was still in her living room, and boy, was it late. She wanted to tell her friends Lexi and Valerie about the proposal immediately, but that just wasn’t possible. They both had husbands, and she wasn’t going to disturb their households at this hour. She thought about calling her parents, who she knew wouldn’t mind the late interruption for this kind of news. She dug out her phone and got all the way to her mother’s contact page before changing her mind and putting it away. It wasn’t right to tell them this news over the phone. She’d go see them tomorrow and tell them in person, she decided. She needed to be up for church the next morning but was just too excited to sleep, so she headed into the kitchen for a snack. She always kept a basket full of junk food on the counter so she’d have something easy to grab that tasted good. This time, a bag of barbecue chips caught her eye, so she sat at her kitchen table and ripped them open. As Sophie ate, she thought about all the changes that would come to her life when she became Mrs. Kemper. Maybe this would give her the opportunity to pursue the degree in early childhood education she’d wanted, but now wasn’t the time to think about that. She’d talk it over with Zach later. They’d make the decision together once she was Zach’s wife. Zach’s wife—it had such a beautiful ring to it. And the ring itself was gorgeous, a princess-cut diamond on white gold with tiny diamonds around it. He’d explained that the wedding band fit in perfectly and added a couple of gold leaves to the design. He’d had it a long time, waiting until they were both certain their momentary breakup was never going to be repeated. She was so glad he’d finally put that ring on her finger. The breakup had been so hard on both of them. He was going through a horrible time, finding out that a man he’d called his best friend had been responsible for the death of a woman, and because the circumstances were so similar, and this man had been the only one present in his late wife’s last moments, it appeared the friend had also killed her. It was a painful time he would never forget, but then, God’s love was helping him heal. As she popped the next chip in her mouth, a rush of panic flowed through her veins. She loved the idea of a Christmas wedding, but that was only two months and a few days away. *Was it even possible to plan a wedding so quickly?* Though Zach had been married before, this would be Sophie’s one and only wedding day. She wanted it to be incredible by wearing a beautiful dress and having all her friends and family there sharing this day with them. She hoped the quick timeline wasn’t going to be a problem. Had they stayed “in the line” of all their friends getting engaged, they would have been married about three weddings ago. Maybe it was more. She wasn’t sure. Somehow, the group of friends had started entering serious relationships at the same time, and at first they’d seemed to have made an agreement to get engaged in the order they’d gotten together as couples. But Sophie and Zach’s breakup messed up the whole thing. Some of the men even delayed their proposals, hoping Zach would patch things up and keep to the right order, but eventually, they had to move on with their lives, and neither Zach nor Sophie blamed them. They’d attended many of those weddings as they worked on patching up their relationship, and finally, Zach had opened his heart completely to God and found his way back to her. She finished the chips and washed them down with some fruit punch, deciding to forgo the caffeinated soda. She needed to at least try to sleep if she was going to be on time for church in the morning. So, she got ready for bed, said another prayer, and settled in under the covers. But then, she couldn’t sleep and found herself just staring up at the ceiling while her heart raced, still thinking about all the plans she had to make in a very short time. Christmas was very close. She had to admit she’d been thinking about her wedding dress for a long time. She had a lot of ideas about what she wanted. She just hoped she’d find something that would tick all those boxes on such short notice. Sophie had also been thinking about the bridal party. She’d been going back and forth about having either Lexi or Valerie as her matron of honor. Now that Valerie was pregnant, it made sense not to put as much pressure on her, and she might still have morning sickness on the wedding day, though she wasn’t sure. That was something she was going to need to look up as well, especially since it was entirely possible she’d experience her own pregnancy next year. *Oh, my goodness,* she thought. *Am I ready for motherhood?* That was something she and Zach hadn’t discussed all day or night after the proposal, though they had talked about it in the past. They’d decided something like that was out of their hands, left for God to decide. They’d discussed whether they’d pursue IVF as Delaney and Josh had done, and they’d decided they would probably not do that since having a family wasn’t as high on their list as it had been for their friends. But if God saw fit to bless them with children, they would welcome them with pure happiness. She shook those thoughts from her mind. She could worry about that another day. Now, she needed to sleep so she would be rested and ready to go to church in a few hours. She turned on her side and fluffed her pillow, closing her eyes and determining to get to sleep this time. But then another thought hit her. *What about the venue?* Most people booked their wedding venues at least six months in advance. *What are the chances that something will be available in two months on Christmas Eve Day?* She imagined they were probably slim. Tomorrow was Sunday, and it wasn’t likely anything would be open to book unless they could do it online. Still, she wanted time to look at a few places and see what she and Zach liked. She turned the other way in the bed, fluffing the pillow again and settling in, closing her eyes. But the thoughts just kept coming, this time about moving into Zach’s house. Well, it would be their home then. It was such a beautiful place, an old Victorian five miles out of town that he had spent a lot of money on to fix up. Now, it was stunning, and she’d be adding her own decorative touches, which she’d also been thinking about for a long time. She would miss the small house she’d lived in for so long, but not much. Since Valerie had gotten married and moved out, the place was a little quiet. But then, she’d need to get everything packed in the next two months, which also seemed like a daunting task. So, consumed with thoughts about planning weddings, finding a dress, finding a venue, packing, and handling the hundreds of details she’d need to finalize to be ready for a beautiful wedding, she turned one more time to face the ceiling. “Sophie, go to sleep,” she told herself. Of course, that didn’t work. She shook her head, getting up and turning on the light, knowing that writing things down would help take them out of her mind. Sitting at the desk in her bedroom, she pulled out a notebook and started writing down all the things she needed to remember. At the very top, she wrote, “Talk to Lexi.” She was sure Valerie wouldn’t mind if she chose Lexi as her matron of honor. Lexi knew Sophie very well, so she could help make decisions about things in a way that would suit Sophie’s tastes. Since they worked together at the dental office, she could also keep her calm and focused on her patients on those days when the excitement would inevitably be too much for her. Next, she made a list of the design she wanted for her wedding dress—a pretty full skirt, lots of lace, and a sweetheart neckline. But then she remembered it would be Christmas, so maybe she’d need something different because it might be cold and snowing. Of course, she didn’t know whether her wedding would be outside or inside, because they hadn’t chosen a venue, but she hoped she could have the outdoor wedding she’d always dreamed of. She wrote down her thoughts about the venue and her plan for the move, not that anything would be set in stone, so she could get it all out of her mind, knowing it was somewhere written down in black and white. First thing in the morning, she would talk to Lexi. But then she remembered church. Lexi and her husband Max would attend their church in Shepherdstown where they lived. She and Zach would go to First Christian in Charles Town, so she’d need to find another way to tell her about the proposal and ask her to be her matron of honor. After taking a few deep breaths and saying another prayer, this one thanking God again for bringing Zach to her as well as asking for His help keeping herself relaxed, she turned off the light again and headed back to bed. This time, she snuggled under the blankets and closed her eyes. Her peaceful mind relaxed as she drifted off to sleep. ## Chapter 2 *Zach* “How did you sleep?” Zach asked when he picked her up the next morning. She let out a chuckle before quieting down to explain. “It was bad. I had a million wedding planning thoughts pouring through my mind. I think I got about four hours at most. I’m sorry we’re going to miss Sunday School class.” “It’s okay. I didn’t sleep well, either. I think I got about three hours,” he admitted, and they both laughed together. He opened her car door and closed it behind her with both hands. “We need to get the venue figured out,” she said when he got into the driver’s seat. “I really doubt there'll be much availability with two-months’ notice.” “You’re probably right, but I’m guessing that’s something we can’t do today,” he said. She shook her head. “Probably not, but as soon as I get Lexi on board, we’re on it.” He drove a bit longer and pulled into the church parking lot. “Are we telling everyone?” “Not before church.” She looked down at her ring. “Though I doubt I can keep this ring a secret long.” “We’ll tell them at lunch,” he said. “Okay,” she said softly. “I’d like to go talk to Mom and Dad this afternoon.” “Of course,” he agreed. “We’ll drive out after we eat.” His parents, as well as his brother and his wife, lived in St. Louis. He was planning on delivering the news to them on a video call. He glanced over after parking and noticed the look in her eyes as she played with the ring. With that, he felt a smile grow on his face. He’d been crazy for breaking up with her. He knew that. But those dark emotions inside during the trial made him feel like the kind of man who could never be a good husband to a woman like Sophie. Thankfully, he’d reached out to God for help, and His light and love brought him back to her. And now, it was time to think about Him some more as he took Sophie’s hand—the one with the ring—and walked into the First Christian sanctuary. He always felt a sense of peace stepping into God’s house, but today, after finally proposing to Sophie the day before, the sensation was even stronger. Before they were seated, they ran into their friends Casey and Isabelle, who were recently married and had returned from their honeymoon in the Maldives not long ago. The thought made Zach wonder where he and Sophie might go for their own honeymoon. He liked the idea of a tropical island with miles of white sandy beaches and clear blue ocean water. He knew they’d need to plan that soon as well, but he hadn’t wanted to bother Sophie with it yet because she seemed so nervous about the wedding planning. “Good morning!” Isabelle greeted them warmly. “Good morning,” Sophie said. “It looks like we have a big crowd here today.” Zach looked around. It did seem like there were more people in the church than usual, but that happened sometimes. There were a lot of churches in Charles Town, but First Christian was the largest, so not everyone in the congregation came every Sunday, unfortunately. There was plenty of room for a full house when that happened, though. They exchanged a few pleasantries with Casey and Isabelle and took a seat next to them just as Pastor Lloyd made his way to the pulpit. Zach gave Sophie’s hand a squeeze as everyone quieted down for the opening prayer. He felt a twinge of giddiness in his heart as he held her hand with that ring, like a schoolboy who knew a secret. The service was beautiful and moving, as it always was. Zach thought about how wonderful it was going to be every Sunday when he woke up with Sophie as his wife and they went to church together then went home together. A bit of that excited schoolboy in him came to the surface again. But he didn’t say anything. Instead, he headed to the car after arranging with Casey to meet at Bishop’s Diner, which they did almost every Sunday. They drove over and headed inside the busy restaurant. As he held out her chair, he noticed Sophie holding her ring hand under the table, no doubt wanting to keep the secret until the announcement. A flash of memory passed through his mind of his late wife, Angel, and the way she’d been excited about their engagement announcement. But then, he shook the thought out of his head. That was the past. His relationship with Sophie was his future, and Angel would want him to be happy, he knew. Two other friends, Sam and Macey Abernathy, walked into the diner, and Sophie waved them over. Zach knew she wanted to talk to Macey right away about flowers since she owned Charles Town Floral. Zach picked up the menu even though he didn’t need to. They’d eaten at Bishop’s more times than he could count, and he knew he wanted the roast beef sandwich. He just wanted the chance to share a knowing glance with Sophie behind the menus before she made her announcement. She did so shortly after everyone ordered. “Hey, everyone. Zach and I have something we want to tell you!” Instead of doing so, she wiggled her fingers in the air. Isabelle caught on first and nearly jumped out of her seat. Everyone laughed, not surprised that the kindergarten teacher was the most animated one in the room. But the rest soon followed, congratulating them. The women stood to give Sophie a hug and the men shook Zach’s hand. Even some friends who’d come in later and sat at another table, Reid and Melody Perry, got in on the action before returning to their children in their booth. “About time,” Sam teased with a whisper as he congratulated Zach. Zach didn’t mind. He knew he’d lagged behind the rest of the friends, but those days were over. “I know,” he whispered back. “I’m glad I finally snapped out of that.” Sam gave him an understanding pat on the shoulder and a look that said he was glad as well. Once they all returned to their seats, Macey looked at Sophie. “Your parents didn’t mention anything at church this morning.” Since Macey and Sam lived in the country, they attended a small church near their home, the same one Sophie’s parents attended. “I haven’t told them yet,” Sophie explained. “We spent all day and night in DC celebrating yesterday, and I wanted to tell them in person. We’re driving out after lunch.” “They’re going to be so happy,” Macey said. “So, when’s the big day?” Zach looked at Sophie, who raised her eyebrows and turned back to Macey. “Well, we’re going to have a Christmas wedding.” “Oh, that’s wonderful,” Macey said. “Melody’s Christmas wedding was so beautiful. I can’t wait for another. Well, that gives you plenty of time to plan, then.” Sophie shook her head. “Actually, it’ll be this Christmas Eve.” The table went silent. “What?” Macey finally asked. “We’re not going to wait,” Zach explained. “So, we’ll get married this Christmas Eve.” “So… we have Kate and Paul’s wedding the next week on New Year’s Eve?” Macey asked. Sophie nodded. “That’s the plan. I know it’s short notice. I hope you can still do our flowers.” “Of course, I can,” Macey said. “I’m just… surprised, and so very happy for you both. This is going to be a wonderful Christmas and New Year’s.” “To Zach and Sophie and a wonderful Christmas wedding.” Sam raised his glass of Dr. Pepper the waitress had just brought him. Zach grabbed his ice water and did the same, and all of them clinked their glasses together in the toast. The conversation turned back to flowers, with Macey obviously excited about getting them together for two weddings on short notice. “What will your colors be?” Macey asked. Sophie looked at Zach before answering. “I was thinking the traditional red and green, probably with silver and gold accents.” “That sounds perfect for a Christmas wedding,” Zach agreed. The friends talked more and finished their lunch together, the women giving each other more hugs before parting ways. Once in the car, Sophie texted her parents and Zach drove them out into the country. Sophie’s parents, Leo and Cindy, didn’t live far out of Charles Town, but Zach knew his fiancée didn’t see them as often as she would have liked. Though they were close, Sophie was an independent person, and her parents always seemed to be busy with their hobbies or volunteer work. Leo had worked for the railroad, and Cindy had worked in a daycare, but they had both since retired, giving their time to the church and their community. They were good people. Zach pulled into their long country driveway, the gravel making crunching sounds under his tires that made him think about winter months when they might sometimes need a four-wheel drive to visit. Once they were married, he knew that would happen a bit more often for his wife’s sake. He knew he’d be happy to listen to Leo’s stories about the railroad, a topic Sophie had said she’d loved as a child. Leo and Cindy were waiting on the porch when Zach parked the car. He walked around to Sophie’s side to be a gentleman, but Sophie was already out and rushing toward her parents before he got there. He chuckled and followed her. “Hi, Mom, Dad,” Sophie said, giving them both warm hugs. “Hello, dear,” Cindy said when she pulled back. “How was church?” “It was beautiful, as always,” Zach answered after shaking Leo’s hand. “One of these days, you’ll have to try a service at Grace Point again,” Leo suggested. “Pastor Jerry delivers a fine sermon.” “We will do that,” Zach promised. He thought it would be a good idea that might give Sophie more time with her parents. Both churches were Christian, and he was sure God blessed both congregations equally, so it was worth a try. He’d heard it was a small, tight-knit group who came together there every Sunday. “I’m afraid we’ve already had lunch,” Cindy said. “I do have some leftover lemon pie we could share, though.” “You know I’d love that. We already ate, too,” Zach said. Cindy’s lemon meringue pie was almost what one would call famous in Charles Town. “Well, come on in.” Leo held the door open while his wife, daughter, and the man he thought was just his daughter’s boyfriend entered. They sat at the kitchen table, and Sophie helped her mother get the pie plated. Once she was seated, Zach noticed she had turned her ring around, probably so it wouldn’t be as noticeable before the announcement, he thought. “Are you two out for a country drive?” Cindy asked, taking a bite and looking expectantly at them. “Not really,” Sophie answered. “We wanted to come talk to you.” “Oh? Is something wrong?” Her father crinkled his brow. “No, just the opposite,” Sophie said with a huge grin. “Zach proposed!” She flipped her ring around and held her hand out to her mother. While they were busy gushing, Leo gave Zach a wink. He had already talked to her father, even though he knew Sophie was independent enough to make her own decisions. He’d still thought it was best to keep up the old-fashioned tradition of talking to the woman’s father first before proposing. Leo had offered some valuable advice that day. Of course, he had also happily given his blessing. Leo rose and gave his daughter a warm hug then came over to shake Leo’s hand again. “I’m so happy for you both.” “We’ve been praying for this for so long,” Cindy admitted. “So, have you set a date yet?” Leo asked. “Dear, I’m sure they need a little time first,” Cindy said. “Actually, Mom, Dad, we’re planning on getting married this Christmas Eve,” Sophie explained. They both looked at each other then back at Sophie and Zach one at a time. “Well, that’s sudden, but not completely shocking,” Cindy said finally. “Congratulations! We’re both so excited. Have you chosen the venue yet?” Sophie shook her head. “Not yet, but that’s on the list for tomorrow.” “Good, you’ll need to snatch one up quickly.” Cindy pushed her pie plate away and started talking to her daughter about the wedding plans. “Well, son,” Leo said, turning to Zach. “We’ve been waiting a long time to officially welcome you into the family. I’m beyond pleased that it will be happening soon.” “So am I.” Zach felt his heart beam with warmth.
